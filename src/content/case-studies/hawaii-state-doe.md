# State of Hawaiʻi Department of Education

## The Organization

The Hawaiʻi State Department of Education runs public education for the whole state as a single statewide district, serving students on every major island. Because Hawaiʻi is one district rather than many, a curriculum decision made at the department level can reach schools across the state at once. That structure is what makes department approval matter so much for any program written for young people.

## The Engagement

The relationship took about two years to build. In the fall of 2024 Michele met the department's former retired Deputy Superintendent, Phyllis Unebasami, who told her the entire state of Hawaiʻi public schools needed this material. That introduction led to a December 2024 presentation of the Brave Series to Superintendent Hayashi, the head of the Hawaiʻi DOE, who said he wanted to roll it out statewide.

One obstacle stood in the way. Public schools do not separate boys and girls, and the Brave Series had been built as separate editions for each. So Michele and her team paused Brave & Bold and put the gas pedal on a co-ed edition. Phyllis Unebasami joined the team for a full year and wrote all of the teacher lessons for Brave Together Coed.

Alongside the curriculum work, Michele presented the Brave Series to all of Hawaiʻi's middle school principals at a statewide meeting, and flew to Maui to present the Brave Series and the Dream Big Journal to counselors from several DOE districts.

## The Outcome

In the summer of 2026, Brave Together Coed was vetted and approved by the State of Hawaiʻi for use in secondary public schools, with each school opting in for itself. One middle school is planning to pilot it this school year. The principals' meeting drew a strong positive reaction, and the Maui counselors bought Dream Big Journals for every counselor in the room, which is where that working relationship began.

## Michele's take

"I met the former retired Deputy Superintendent of the Hawaiʻi Department of Education. She said she believed the entire state of Hawaiʻi public schools needed this. I got to present it to the current Superintendent Hayashi. After my presentation, he said he wanted to roll it out statewide."
