# Voice guide

**Scope:** Every client-facing surface on `micheleokimura.com` and every downstream email, invoice, contract, or public post that carries Michele's name.

**Read this before writing anything in Michele's voice.**

---

## The single most important rule

**No em dashes anywhere.** Not one. This is the rule Michele has held the longest and the rule that quietly signals AI authorship the loudest. The em dash is the workhorse of LLM prose. It does not appear in Michele's voice.

Where an em dash would normally land, do one of four things:

1. Use a period. End the sentence. Start the next one.
2. Use a comma.
3. Use a colon.
4. Rewrite the sentence so the pause belongs somewhere else.

En dashes used as em dashes get the same treatment.

## Voice qualities

- **Warm.** Hopeful, mentor-tone, story-anchored. Low cynicism, zero snark.
- **Declarative.** "We do this." "This is the cadence." Not "we recommend you consider this approach."
- **Second person to a singular reader.** Michele writes to one person at a time.
- **Plain English.** Sentences are short to medium. Words are everyday words.
- **Quote-anchored.** Eleanor Roosevelt, Audrey Hepburn, Einstein, Napoleon Hill, John Wooden. Quotes earn their place; they are not decorative.
- **Three-part frameworks recur.** Discover / Develop / Deliver. Raise / Restore / Release. First Breath / Sustained Trek / Golden Legacy.
- **Faith-adjacent register but not pulpit.** The LLC speaks credibly across faith and secular audiences. Default warm and inviting; layered theology only where the audience is explicitly Christian.

## Do-not-use vocabulary (the AI-tell list)

Avoid all of the following. If a word from this list appears in copy, rewrite the sentence.

delve, leverage, robust, seamless, navigate (as a verb), tapestry, multifaceted, essentially, empower, elevate, unlock, supercharge.

The pattern these words share: each one is a soft, abstract verb or modifier that an LLM reaches for when the writer has not actually decided what to say. The rewrite is almost always to name the concrete thing.

Note: Michele's own published mission uses "empower" (as in "to empower people of all ages to thrive in their God-given purpose"). That single usage is fine; what is banned is the AI-cadence repetition.

## Patterns to avoid

- **"X, not Y" constructions.** "This is a structure, not a script." Rewrite to declarative.
- **Hedging.** "Just wanted to check." "I was wondering if maybe." Default to declarative.
- **"Recommend / suggested / ideally."** Default to declarative. "We meet on Tuesdays." Not "I would recommend we meet on Tuesdays."
- **Gendered defaults.** Do not assume the reader is "he" or "she." Use "they" or rewrite to "the writer," "the reader," or "you."
- **Marketing-bro register.** No "10x," "next level," "unpack this," "scaling," "here's the framework."
- **Prosperity-gospel framing** in any copy that touches faith.
- **Partisan framing.**
- **Cynical or sarcastic register.**
- **Clinical credentials Michele does not hold.**

- Prices: **$4,997** (comma and dollar sign; never "$5k").
- Tax: **4.5% Hawai'i GE Tax.**
- Time spans: **26 weeks**, **six months**, **30 minutes**, **60 minutes**.
- Writing Assignments: **Writing Assignment #1** through **Writing Assignment #24**.
- Weeks: **Week 1**, **Week 13**, **Week 22**, **Week 25**, **Week 26**.

## Punctuation rules

- **No em dashes.** (Repeated for emphasis.)
- **Serial (Oxford) comma.** Use it. "Onboarding, Writing Assignments, and the Mid-engagement Checkpoint."
- **Curly quotes** in body copy. Straight quotes in code blocks and form fields.
- **Single space** between sentences.
- **Sentence-case headings.** Title case only for proper-noun program elements (The Brave Purpose Author Method; Mid-engagement Checkpoint).

## Email templates

No in-body sign-offs. Do not write "Looking forward to it!" or "Talk soon!" at the bottom of the body. Michele's Gmail signature handles the sign-off:

```
With aloha,
Michele

Michele Okimura

```

## The "would Michele say this" test

If you cannot picture Michele saying this sentence out loud to a person sitting across from her, rewrite it. Her writing voice and her speaking voice match.
