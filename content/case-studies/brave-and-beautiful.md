## Positioning

A leadership curriculum for teen girls focused on identity, worth, confidence, resiliency, and protection from exploitation. Faith-based at its core, designed to function in public-school settings, private schools, church youth groups, and community programs alike.

---

## Origin story

Michele served as Creative Director, Chief Editor, and lead author for *Brave & Beautiful*. The curriculum grew out of her Explicit Movement work and the recurring question she heard from parents, teachers, and youth workers across three continents: "How do we equip teen girls with the tools to know their worth and stand in it?"

The answer became a multi-lesson curriculum built around the four working ideas at the heart of the Brave Series:

1. Unlock purpose.
2. Prevent exploitation.
3. Establish identity and self-worth.
4. Build resiliency, confidence, and skills to live a meaningful life.

*Brave & Beautiful* landed first in the series (2022), followed by *Brave & Bold* for teen boys and *Brave Together* for coed settings.

---

## Structure and methodology

- Lesson-based structure with a companion slide deck for each lesson.
- Built for adaptable delivery: single-day retreat, weekly small group, semester-long course, or in-school elective.
- Each lesson pairs a story-anchored teaching with a hands-on activity or reflection.
- Language is age-appropriate for teen girls; register moves between plain-spoken warmth and direct challenge.

The pattern that connects this curriculum to the Brave Purpose Author Method: Michele architects a body of teaching as a set of chapters that stand alone AND cohere as a whole. Every writer she coaches learns this two-level design (individual chapter integrity plus whole-book coherence).

## What Michele learned that now feeds the Method

*Brave & Beautiful* taught Michele how to write for a reader who was not going to read alone. Curriculum has a teacher between the writer and the student. Trade books mostly do not. But the discipline of writing content that lands cleanly across many teaching hands transferred directly to trade-book writing: chapters have to stand on their own, and openings and endings have to do a disproportionate share of the work. She teaches this in the Method.
